# John Deere’s Digital Pivot: Can a 187-Year-Old Manufacturer Become a Tech Platform?

## Introduction

In early 2023, John Deere CEO John May faced a defining question: could a company known for tractors and heavy machinery successfully reinvent itself as a technology platform company?

Deere had already invested heavily into precision agriculture, AI-driven automation, and cloud-connected farm equipment. But tensions were mounting. Farmers were pushing back against software restrictions, regulators were raising concerns about data ownership, and competitors were accelerating their own digital investments.

As May described the shift, “We’re really a technology company at this point.” The company’s future no longer depended on steel and horsepower—it depended on data.

## The Protagonist: John May

John May became CEO of John Deere in 2019 after nearly two decades with the company. With a background in both finance and operations, May was tasked with leading Deere through one of the most significant transformations in its history.

Under his leadership, Deere began positioning itself not just as an equipment provider, but as a technology-driven solutions company focused on data, automation, and precision agriculture.

## The Digital Transformation

John Deere’s transformation centered around precision agriculture — a system that uses data, sensors, GPS, and artificial intelligence to optimize farming operations.

Key investments included:
- Autonomous tractors capable of operating without human drivers, publicly unveiled at CES 2022  
- Acquisition of Blue River Technology in 2017 to expand machine learning capabilities in agriculture  
- Development of the John Deere Operations Center, a cloud-based platform used by farmers to manage field data  
- Expansion of precision agriculture offerings, which now represent a major portion of Deere’s Production & Precision Agriculture segment  

These innovations allowed farmers to increase efficiency, reduce costs, and improve crop yields. However, they also fundamentally changed Deere’s business model.

Instead of a one-time equipment sale, Deere began shifting toward recurring revenue through software and data services, aligning with broader industry trends toward digital platforms.

## Strategic Tensions

Despite the promise of digital transformation, several challenges emerged.

### Hardware vs Software Identity
Deere had built its reputation as a manufacturing powerhouse. Transitioning to a software-driven company raised questions about whether it could compete with technology firms while maintaining its engineering advantage.

### Data Ownership Concerns
Farmers became increasingly concerned about who owned the data generated by their equipment and how it could be used. As agriculture becomes more data-intensive, control over data becomes a competitive and ethical issue.

### Right-to-Repair Movement
Deere faced backlash for restricting access to repair software. Farmers argued that they should have the right to fix their own equipment, while Deere maintained that restrictions were necessary to ensure safety and protect intellectual property.

### High Investment Costs
The company invested heavily into digital capabilities, including AI, automation, and cloud infrastructure. However, adoption rates varied, and the long-term return on these investments remained uncertain.

## Industry Context

Agriculture is undergoing a major transformation driven by labor shortages, rising costs, and the need for higher efficiency.

By 2023, John Deere’s Production & Precision Agriculture segment had become one of the company’s largest business units, reflecting a significant shift toward digital and data-driven services.

Industry analysts note that “the future of farming is increasingly data-driven,” with technology playing a central role in improving productivity and sustainability.

Technology has become a key differentiator, with companies racing to provide digital solutions that improve productivity.

## The Decision Point

By 2023, John May and his leadership team faced a critical decision: how aggressively should Deere pursue its transformation into a technology platform?

Moving too slowly risked falling behind competitors. Moving too quickly risked alienating customers and overextending investments.

The path forward was uncertain.

## Discussion Questions

1. Should John Deere fully transition to a software-driven business model, or maintain its identity as a manufacturing company?
2. How should Deere address farmer concerns around data ownership and control?
3. Is Deere’s closed ecosystem strategy sustainable in the face of “right to repair” pressures?
4. What risks does Deere face in investing heavily in autonomous and AI-driven farming technologies?
5. How should Deere balance short-term profitability with long-term digital transformation investments?
